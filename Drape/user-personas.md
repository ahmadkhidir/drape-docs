# User Personas

## Overview

The Drape customer base is segmented along two primary axes: purchasing intent (personal custom-occasionwear buyers vs. professional couture practitioners) and garment structure (flowing draped occasionwear vs. structured architectural tailoring). While their aesthetic requirements vary from bridal brocades to worsted suiting wools, all personas are united by high-stakes purchasing where cutting non-returnable luxury textiles demands precise pre-visualization.

## Persona 1: Amara Okafor

**Tagline:** High-society occasionwear client seeking certainty before cutting luxury bridal and cultural couture textiles.
**Demographic:** 32 years old, London / Lagos, Senior Tech Product Marketing Director, Master's Degree.
**Role:** Bride-to-be and bespoke cultural occasionwear patron.
**Tech Comfort:** High
**Research basis:** [Reddit r/weddingplanning custom dress discussions](https://www.reddit.com/r/weddingplanning/); [Bridal and Aso Ebi couture consumer interviews](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQGO_M9H11z4JN7umWd6S8jzF0ZyJ9nBjH9UOy5Guh7epgOGuM5MDmNyD49GrU7vW4HEaqPLZ_4ZApwN8p1S1bTmcCkumBowJOsUn8BMa0Ff5WTH-gIqZyBce9qkOHR-HTBJQLOS7JFHUjqVmDubF45G6k2ku9PkKWNs8qCLtuqbp7PvIMRrSvwv6Q==).
**In their words:** "I am spending £700 on hand-woven silk jacquard for my traditional wedding reception. If the dressmaker cuts it and the silhouette makes me look bulky instead of sculpted, that money is completely gone and my wedding look is ruined."

### Goals
- Visualize how a specific raw fabric bolt (weight, sheen, and weave) will drape over her actual body proportions before purchase.
- Test multiple garment silhouettes (e.g., corseted mermaid, structured off-shoulder gown, cape dress) against the same selected textile.
- Generate an authoritative, technical yardage estimation and specification card to hand directly to her private dressmaker.
- Order physical luxury swatches to verify tactile hand-feel and skin-tone harmony before placing the final yardage order.

### Frustrations
- Traditional 2"x2" swatches mailed in envelopes provide zero indication of how 5 yards of fabric will fall, crease, or catch light when moving.
- Tailors frequently overestimate required yardage "just in case," leading to costly wasted surplus on £120/yard fabrics.
- Online fabric stores only show flat product photography pinned to walls or rolled bolts with no human context.
- Fear of irreversible cutting: luxury fabric cut to custom lengths is strictly non-returnable across the entire textile industry.

### Behaviors
- Curates Pinterest boards and Instagram saved collections of bespoke celebrity gowns and runway silhouettes.
- Shops primarily on mobile and laptop late in the evening after work, sharing candidate fabrics and screenshots with family via WhatsApp.
- Employs private dressmakers rather than purchasing off-the-rack designer clothing because standard sizing does not flatter her hourglass proportions.

### Scenario
Amara is preparing for her wedding reception and wants a bespoke dress made from heavy emerald silk brocade. She visits Drape on her iPad, finds an Italian silk brocade with gold metallic threads, and uses the real-time WebGPU sandbox to pull and wave the virtual cloth to inspect its stiffness. She then uploads a well-lit full-body photo to the Neural Tailor, selects the "Architectural Corset Mermaid" silhouette, and within four seconds sees a photorealistic preview of herself in the gown with authentic fabric sheen. Reassured that the metallic weave accentuates rather than overwhelms her shape, she orders the exact 4.5 yards recommended by the yardage calculator and downloads the technical spec card for her dressmaker.

## Persona 2: Julien Moreau

**Tagline:** Independent boutique couture director looking to accelerate private client consultations and eliminate material prototyping waste.
**Demographic:** 38 years old, Paris, Creative Director & Atelier Founder, Fashion Design Degree.
**Role:** Independent fashion designer and bespoke atelier owner.
**Tech Comfort:** Medium
**Research basis:** [Business of Fashion independent designer reports](https://www.businessoffashion.com); [Couture atelier textile sourcing forums](https://fabrics-fabrics.com).
**In their words:** "Explaining to a private client why a 400 GSM silk gazar behaves differently from a 120 GSM silk crepe de chine takes hours of conversation and sketch revisions. If they could see the cloth simulated on their silhouette instantly, closing commissions would take minutes."

### Goals
- Inspect microscopic weave construction, thread count, and directional light refraction of designer textiles without waiting weeks for sample books.
- Conduct interactive, high-fidelity digital client consultations where fabrics and silhouettes can be swapped live.
- Accurately calculate pattern layout and fabric consumption for complex, bias-cut garments to minimize cutting room scrap.
- Secure exclusive or limited-run heritage deadstock rolls with guaranteed fiber purity certification.

### Frustrations
- Client indecision: private couture clients struggle to read flat 2D fashion sketches and imagine the finished physical drape.
- High cost of physical muslin prototyping: creating physical toile samples for every fabric candidate drains atelier labor and budget.
- Unreliable fabric specs from wholesale distributors regarding true GSM, shrinkage, and warp/weft grain behavior.
- Traditional luxury showrooms require in-person visits to Paris, Milan, or New York, consuming precious studio production time.

### Behaviors
- Operates on a dual-monitor desktop workstation during studio hours and presents client lookbooks on an iPad Pro during private fittings.
- Relies heavily on technical textile terminology (GSM, momme, warp/weft tension, weave structure) when evaluating materials.
- Purchases in bolt increments (10 to 30 yards) for private capsule runs once a textile passes client approval.

### Scenario
Julien is meeting with a prominent client who has commissioned a sculptural evening coat for an awards gala. Sitting together in his studio with Drape open on a studio monitor, Julien pulls up a curated Italian double-faced cashmere. He activates the Micro-Weave Inspector, panning a directional light across the surface to demonstrate the matte luster of the weave. He selects an oversized architectural coat silhouette and maps the client's dimensions, demonstrating how the heavy 520 GSM wool maintains its sharp shoulder crease without collapsing. The client is immediately convinced, and Julien orders the required 6 yards along with the grainline layout blueprint.

## Persona 3: Marcus Sterling

**Tagline:** Discerning suiting collector seeking bespoke tailoring perfection without the guesswork of traditional swatch books.
**Demographic:** 45 years old, New York / Chicago, Private Equity Partner, JD/MBA.
**Role:** Bespoke tailoring patron and collector.
**Tech Comfort:** High
**Research basis:** [Styleforum bespoke suiting discussions](https://www.styleforum.net); [Bespoke tailoring customer surveys](https://britexfabrics.com).
**In their words:** "A two-inch square swatch tells me nothing about how a chalk-stripe flannel will drape across my shoulders or break over my shoes. I want to see the suit on a body with my build before my tailor cuts into a $300-a-yard bolt."

### Goals
- See how distinctive suiting patterns (herringbone, Glen plaid, windowpane) scale and align across a tailored silhouette on his athletic frame.
- Verify the seasonal weight, breathability, and wrinkle resistance of worsted wools, linens, and silk-wool blends.
- Seamlessly share fabric orders and mill specifications with his master tailor on Savile Row or in Manhattan.
- Maintain an organized digital wardrobe archive of purchased fabrics and tailoring specifications for reordering.

### Frustrations
- Tailor swatch books are tiny, bound scraps with heavy paper backing that mask the real drape and recovery of the cloth.
- Misaligned expectations: discovering only at the second fitting that a selected fabric pattern reads louder or falls differently than imagined.
- Fragmented communication between fabric merchants and busy tailors regarding dye lots and roll delivery timelines.

### Behaviors
- Browses high-end tailoring blogs and menswear forums on desktop; orders during business travel via smartphone.
- Values understated luxury, craftsmanship provenance, and transparent mill heritage (e.g., Biella or Huddersfield mills).
- Values time efficiency and is willing to pay premium prices for flawless, frictionless service.

### Scenario
Marcus is commissioning a three-piece summer suit for an international summit. He browses Drape's suiting curation on his laptop and filters for high-twist Irish linen and tropical wool blends. He isolates a midnight-navy Solaro fabric and uses the interactive sandbox to observe its subtle two-tone color shift under changing virtual light angles. Entering his height and suiting measurements, he views a 3D tailored representation of the cloth rendered as a double-breasted suit. Satisfied with the pattern scale, he purchases 4 yards and inputs his tailor's studio address for direct white-glove delivery with an attached digital cutting sheet.

## Anti-Personas

### Anti-Persona 1: Chloe Bennett — The Fast-Fashion DIY Crafter
**Role:** Casual weekend hobbyist sewist making quick tote bags, scrunchies, and children's Halloween costumes.
**Why we do not serve them:** Chloe prioritizes bargain prices ($3–$8/yard synthetic fleece, quilting cotton, and polyester blends). Drape's inventory is strictly natural, luxury, and heritage textiles ($50–$300+/yard) with a non-returnable cut policy that does not fit low-budget trial-and-error hobby sewing.
**Feature requests to decline:** Bulk discount bins, polyester novelty prints, $1 bargain remnant sales, and flexible 30-day return policies on cut yardage.

### Anti-Persona 2: Derek Vance — Industrial Apparel Sourcing Broker
**Role:** Mass-production sourcing agent procuring thousands of meters of commodity polyester/cotton uniform twill for factory assembly lines.
**Why we do not serve them:** Derek requires container-load shipping, wholesale EDI system integrations, and commodity mill certifications at pennies per meter. Drape is an experiential, design-centric atelier serving bespoke cuts and boutique atelier orders, not a B2B shipping exchange.
**Feature requests to decline:** Container-freight EDI dispatch, bulk commodity tenders, uninspected raw greige goods, and sub-$5/meter industrial catalog lines.
